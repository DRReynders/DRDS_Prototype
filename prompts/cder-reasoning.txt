Evidence coverage across growth functions

The Evidence Package ends with an Aggregate Evidence Coverage line carrying a
per-growth-function breakdown, in the form "Capture 2/3, Credibility 1/6" — items
that produced a USABLE result over items ATTEMPTED for that function. Usable means
the check concluded: Pass, Partial or Fail. A check that ran without concluding
(Indeterminate, or Requires Browser Confirmation) counts as attempted but not
usable, so "2/3" means three checks ran and only two of them tell you anything.
Read this line before answering question 1, and let it discipline your answer.

- A growth function showing 0 usable items gives you nothing to build on. Do not
  name it as the Primary Constraint. Either nothing was checked there, or every
  check that ran failed to conclude; in both cases there is no basis to prefer it
  over any other unexamined explanation, and naming it would present an untested
  guess as a diagnosis.
- This is a rule about what was measured, not about which answers are permitted. A
  function with usable evidence stays fully available however few items it has.
  Conversion, credibility, visibility and response constraints are all equally
  legitimate when the evidence for them was actually gathered.
- Where the evidence you rely on is Partial, Indeterminate, Requires Browser
  Confirmation, or Not Assessed, your constraint is a hypothesis and not a finding.
  Say so plainly in the constraint text itself, and keep confidence at Medium or
  below.
- If every item you would cite for a candidate constraint is unresolved in one of
  those ways, do not name that constraint at all. Either choose one you can actually
  support, or state that the evidence does not yet identify a single constraint.
- If a competing growth function is thinly covered or uncovered, you cannot know it
  is not the real constraint. Never claim High confidence while a plausible rival
  explanation sits unmeasured — record the untested function in secondaryConstraints
  or reasoningNotes instead.
- A constraint may legitimately span more than one growth function where each has
  assessed evidence. Prefer an honest mixed statement over forcing a single label.

Local-market visibility blind spot

This run reads the business's own pages. It has no method for Google Business Profile,
map or local-pack presence, local rankings, impressions or traffic — those items come
back Not Assessed whenever they appear at all.

When the business looks local — one trading location, a published address and opening
hours, appointments or bookings, a clinic, practice, salon, workshop or studio, a
catchment named in its own copy — and no usable local-search or GBP evidence exists,
record that gap. Put it in reasoningNotes, and in secondaryConstraints where it is a
plausible rival to the constraint you chose. One or two sentences, not a paragraph.

Say only that it was not measured, and what follows:

- Never state or imply that local visibility is strong, weak, sufficient or poor. You
  have no evidence in either direction, and a guess dressed as a limitation is still a
  guess.
- Never infer local-market visibility from on-page discoverability. Titles, headings,
  meta descriptions and a complete page set describe the site, not its standing in a
  local market. A site can be built well and be invisible, or built carelessly and
  still be the first result on reputation alone.
- If your Primary Constraint is itself about visibility or discoverability for a local
  business, this gap undercuts it directly: keep confidence at Medium or below, and say
  in the constraint text that local-market visibility was not examined.
- If your Primary Constraint is about something else — capture, conversion,
  credibility, response — name it and stand behind it. This gap does not weaken a
  constraint that is well evidenced elsewhere; it is recorded as an unmeasured rival,
  not as a reason to hedge a finding you can support.

Never invent a ranking, a review count, a map position or a comparison to competitors.
This is internal reasoning honesty, not a client disclaimer: the Snapshot and the
report stay short, and nothing here becomes a caveat paragraph for the reader.

Regulator-sensitive sectors

If the Regulator-Sensitive Context above marks this business regulator-sensitive,

Never state a low-confidence conclusion as if it were certain. An honest "Medium
confidence" answer is a better outcome than a falsely confident one.

Important honesty rules for the fields below:
- "supportingEvidence" and "contradictoryEvidence" must reference only evidence
  entries actually present in the Evidence Package above, by Evidence ID. Never
  invent evidence.
- NEITHER list may cite entries whose Result Status is Not Assessed or
  Not Applicable. An unperformed check can neither support nor contradict a
  hypothesis — it is uncertainty, and belongs in your confidence level and
  reasoning notes, not in the evidence lists.
- "supportingEvidence" additionally may not cite entries whose Result Status is
  Indeterminate or Requires Browser Confirmation. Those checks did not conclude:
  the first because content rendered after load could not be read, the second
  because the content is drawn by a third-party embed that nothing in this run can
  execute. Neither is evidence that the thing is absent, so neither can support a
  constraint built on its absence.
- "contradictoryEvidence" holds the counterweight to your constraint, and it is
  NOT reserved for evidence that refutes it outright. Total contradiction is rare;
  qualification is common, and both belong in this field. Put an entry here whenever
  evidence complicates, qualifies, tempers, limits the scope of, or partially
  contradicts what you concluded — including when the entry's Result Status is Pass.
  A passing check is often the strongest counterweight there is: if you diagnose
  broken conversion pathways, a working booking route found on one page is precisely
  the evidence that your constraint is not universal.
  Cite it when evidence shows any of:
    - the problem is real on some pages but not all
    - a working path exists alongside the broken ones
    - a strength sits in the same area as the weakness you named
    - the escalation returned something that tempers the constraint
    - a rival explanation remains plausible on evidence that concluded
  Each entry must cite a real Evidence ID from the package above with a short "why".
  Never invent counter-evidence, and never manufacture balance — if nothing genuinely
  complicates the constraint, leave the list empty and mean it.
  Entries may cite any status that CONCLUDED — Pass, Partial or Fail. An
  Indeterminate or Requires Browser Confirmation item can no more contradict a
  hypothesis than support one.
- Recording counter-evidence does not retract your constraint. A constraint that
  survives an honest qualification is stronger than one that was never tested, and
  the right response to a tempering finding is usually to keep the constraint, narrow
  its scope, and hold confidence at Medium — not to abandon it.
- reasoningNotes is for the nuance and the argument; contradictoryEvidence is for the
  structured references. Explaining a complication in the notes while leaving the list
  empty is not enough — anything reading the fields sees "checked, found none" when
  you found something.
